Light comes in packets of energy called photons, and the energy of each one is
set by a single property: its frequency. Type a value into either field above
and the other updates instantly. Numbers like `5.45e14` work too.

## The formula

The Planck relation, also called the Planck–Einstein relation, is:

$$
E = h\nu
$$

Here <Katex tex="E" /> is the photon's energy, <Katex tex="\nu" /> (the Greek letter nu) is its frequency, and <Katex tex="h" /> is the Planck constant:

$$
h = 6.62607015 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}
$$

To go the other way, divide by <Katex tex="h" />:

$$
\nu = \frac{E}{h}
$$

The relation is a straight proportion, so doubling the frequency doubles the energy.

## Worked example

Green light has a frequency of about <Katex tex="5.45 \times 10^{14}" /> Hz, so one photon carries:

$$
E = (6.626 \times 10^{-34})(5.45 \times 10^{14})
$$

$$
E \approx 3.61 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}
$$

Dividing by <Katex tex="1.602 \times 10^{-19}" /> joules per electronvolt gives about 2.25 eV.

## Reading the photon drawing

The moving wave packet is a schematic. Raise the frequency and its waves pack
more tightly, and the marker slides right along the spectrum bar. The number of
waves drawn isn't to scale, since real frequencies span far too wide a range to
draw literally. The packet's height stays the same because a single photon's
energy depends only on its frequency. A brighter light of the same colour has
more photons, not more energetic ones.
