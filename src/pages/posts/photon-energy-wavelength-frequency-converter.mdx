A photon's energy, wavelength, and frequency aren't three
independent properties — they're three ways of describing
the same thing. Fix any one of them and the other two follow
immediately. That single relationship is why an X-ray
telescope reports counts in keV while a radio telescope
reports frequency in GHz and an optical astronomer talks in
nanometres: it's all the same electromagnetic spectrum,
just read off in whichever unit is most convenient for that
regime.

## The physics

Two equations connect all three quantities. First, every wave obeys the universal relation between wavelength, frequency, and the speed of light:

$$
c = \lambda f
$$

Second, Planck's relation ties a photon's energy directly to its frequency (equivalently, its wavelength):

$$
E = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda}, \qquad h \approx 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}
$$

Because <Katex tex="E" /> and <Katex tex="f" /> both scale directly with each other, and <Katex tex="\lambda" /> scales inversely with them, higher-energy photons always mean shorter wavelengths and higher frequencies — a gamma ray isn't a "stronger" radio wave, it's the same phenomenon at the opposite end of an enormous logarithmic scale. The converter above applies these two equations both ways, so editing any of the three fields instantly recomputes the other two.
