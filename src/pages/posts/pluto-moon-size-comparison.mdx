<h2>The numbers behind the scale</h2>

<p>The Moon is about 3,475 km across. Pluto is about 2,377 km across. The ratio of their diameters is:</p>

$$
\frac{D_{Moon}}{D_{Pluto}} = \frac{3{,}475}{2{,}377} \approx 1.46
$$

<p>Volume scales with the cube of the diameter:</p>

$$
(1.46)^3 \approx 3.1
$$

<p>So you could fit about three Plutos inside the Moon. Surface area, which sets how big each world looks, scales with the square:</p>

$$
(1.46)^2 \approx 2.1
$$

<p>The Moon's face is about twice the size of Pluto's. The gap in mass is bigger still: the Moon is about 5.6 times as massive, because Pluto is a mix of rock and ice while the Moon is solid rock.</p>

<h2>How small is Pluto?</h2>

<p>Pluto is about half as wide as the United States, coast to coast. Seven moons in the Solar System are larger than it: Jupiter's Ganymede, Callisto, Io and Europa, Saturn's Titan, Neptune's Triton, and our own Moon. That is a big part of why astronomers reclassified it as a dwarf planet in 2006, after finding Eris, a world of almost the same size, even further out.</p>

<p>Pluto does have a moon of its own, Charon, which is about half Pluto's width. The two are so close in size that they orbit a point in the space between them, rather than Charon circling Pluto.</p>
