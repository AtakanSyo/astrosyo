<h2>Review</h2>
<p>SkyQuest XT6 gets close to being the perfect beginner scope if not for its one weakness, and that is its 1.25" focuser. You might think of it as the Achilleus Heel of the SkyQuest XT6.</p>
<p>We will delve into the focuser subject in the accessories section, but before that it needs to be said that, based on owner reports and hands-on reviews, XT6 is one of the best beginner telescopes you can get. The optical power is more than enough for a beginner, the mount is solid, it has balance adjustments, and it doesn't come with useless accessories.</p>
<p>The problem is there is a better model and that is <a href="/posts/skywatcher-6-inch-dobsonian-telescope-review">SkyWatcher 6" Dob.</a> It comes with a 2" focuser, which is a vast improvement in terms of the eyepieces you are able to use with the scope.</p>
<p>Despite the 1.25" focuser, SkyQuest XT6 is an awesome telescope.</p>
<p>Don't buy this great telescope.</p>
<img class="post-image post-image-lg" src={frontmatter.imageDir + '/vertical.webp'} alt="Orion SkyQuest XT6 with the tube pointed straight up"></img>

<h2>Optics</h2>
<p>The optical design of XT6 lean more towards planetary performance with its 6" aperture and 1200mm optical tube. Such a combination will provide an f/8 focal ratio, which is why it leans towards planetary detail. Focusing and getting detail will be easier compared to other Dobs with the SkyQuest XT6. But it sacrifices deep space performance while doing that.</p>
<p>SkyQuest XT6's performance leans towards the Solar System.</p>
<p>The 1.25" focuser combined with the f/8 focal ratio provides a rather narrow field view.</p>

<h3>Planetary and Lunar Performance</h3>
<p>Owners consistently report that planetary detail is SkyQuest XT6's strong suit. It easily extracts detail from Jupiter, Saturn, Mars, Venus and the Moon.</p>
<p>Jupiter's The Great Red Spot, 4 Galilean Moon's and its many stripes are easily visible with Orion SkyQuest XT6.</p>
<p>Rings of Saturn are also easy to see with its Cassini Division, and many moons of Saturn are visible, including its largest moon Titan, which appears as a yellowish dot.</p>
<p>Some surface detail on Mars is visible as black stains and its Polar Ice Caps are noticable as white stains.</p>
<p>Uranus and Neptune will apear as blueis dots, and Neptune's largest moon Triton will be visible.</p>
<p>Phases of Venus are easy to observe with the SkyQuest XT6.</p>
<p>A 6mm Goldline eyepiece will improve planetary performance drastically.</p>
<p>A planetary eyepiece provides exceptional detail with XT6.</p>

<h3>Deep Space Performance</h3>
<p>Deep space performance is not the best a 6" Dob would provide, and this is due to the 1.25" focuser. The field view it provides is narrow, and it makes it harder to observe larger deep space objects. The brightness and sharpness level is the same as any 2" focuser would provide with a 6" telescope, but a wider-field view is a vital improvement for deep space observations. The narrow field view also makes locating objects harder.</p>
<p>But still, <a href="/posts/skywatcher-6-inch-dobsonian-telescope-review">SkyWatcher 6"</a>Dobsonian's deep space performance is better than any refractor or tripod model you would find at this price range.</p>
<p>Do not forget that this is a Dobsonian, which are made for optical performance and nothing else.</p>
<p>A low-powered focal reducer would be ideal for improving deep space performance.</p>
<p>According to owner reports, the brighter Messier Catalog objects are easy targets for Orion SkyQuest XT6. The Andromeda, Orion, Hercules, and the Pleiades are among the mesmerizing objects you can observe. You can get a glimpse of Whirlpool Galaxy and the Crab Nebula, but their extensions appear rather ghostly.</p>
<p>Is it worth it to improve to an8"? Yes, absolutely. The reason is that an 8" would collect %77 more light, improving the image quality by almost two-fold. The difference is almost ridiculous. If you can, you should get an <a href="/posts/zhumell-z8-deluxe-overview">8" Dobsonian</a>. But a 6" will, without a doubt, mesmerize a beginner. So don't get sad if your budget is not enough for an 8”.</p>

<h2>Mount</h2>
<p>The mount is the same as other 6" Dob mounts. Due to its weight and bulky nature it is rock-solid, so you won't have any "stability" problems. The attachments between the optical tube and the mount are rather small, but they won't be problematic with a 6" Dobsonian. They are more of a problem at the 10"-12" range.</p>
<p>Owners describe the base as rock-solid with adequate smoothness.</p>
<p>The Nylon pads that provide the smooth motion are not a premium material. Teflon, is the ultimate, butter smooth material, and we don't understand why Orion uses Nylon. In terms of functionality owners report Nylon pads don't create any problems, but they feel cheap.</p>
<p>The base is particleboard, which is also a cheap material. But we don't mind it for two reasons. Firstly, it is not an important part of the telescope. Secondly, it is made of particle board with even 1500$ Dobsonian's, so this makes it impossible to complain.</p>

<h2>Accessories</h2>
<p>We admire Orion for not including a 10mm Plössl eyepiece. Most people stop using it after a few months since they provide a dim, narrow image. It should be excluded from every telescope package to drive the price down.</p>
<p>The 25mm Plössl eyepiece is decent. It provides 48x magnification with a wide-field view. It is suitable for deep space observations.</p>
<figure class="centered_flex">
  <img class="post-image post-image-lg" src={frontmatter.imageDir + '/finder.webp'} alt="Orion EZ Finder II red dot finder"></img>

<p>Owners describe the red dot finder that comes with the telescope as cheap but functional. It is a simple device. It helps to focus by projecting a red dot on a screen. We think it is better than slightly more expensive scope type finders, 6x30, for example.</p>
<figure class="centered_flex">

<p>The 1.25" focuser choice is disappointing. A f/8 telescope such as the XT6 has a slightly narrow field view, which makes it ideal for planetary detail, but unideal for deep space observations. A 2" focuser would counteract the narrow field view, but Orion doesn't send one. Upgrading the focuser adds to the cost and additional effort.</p>
<p>Unfortunately, this amazing telescope gets pulled down by its focuser.</p>

<h3>Recommended Accessories</h3>
<p>Goldline eyepieces are generally considered a solid step up from Plössl's while staying budget-friendly.</p>
<p>The 9mm will prove much better than any Plössl eyepiece for planetary viewing, providing around 130x magnification. In addition, the 20mm Goldline(60x) is superior in every aspect to the 25mm Plössl eyepiece that comes with the telescope. These upgrades will prove worthy as they will improve the XT6's potential for many years to come.</p>
<img class="post-image post-image-lg" src={frontmatter.imageDir + '/overview.webp'} alt="Orion SkyQuest XT6 Classic Dobsonian"></img>

<h2>Drawbacks</h2>
<p>The 1.25" focuser is the biggest drawback. It should have been a 2". The focuser also deems the tension adjuster springs useless.</p>
<p>The smooth-motion bearings are Nylon instead of Teflon.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>SkyQuest XT6 is not a bad model by any means. In fact, it is one of the best starter telescopes you can get in the whole astronomy world. But, it is not future-proof. You will have to change the focuser eventually, and this will add to the cost.</p>
<p>Until Orion sends a 2" focuser with the XT6, you should get the <a href="/posts/skywatcher-6-inch-dobsonian-telescope-review">SkyWatcher 6".</a></p>
